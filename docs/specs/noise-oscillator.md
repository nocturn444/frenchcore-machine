## 1. What it does for the user

A third sound source, like Serum's noise oscillator: it plays a noise or a short sound alongside the two wavetable oscillators. Use it for grit in the tail, air on top of the punch, or a texture layer you send to its own bus.

- **On**: off by default, so existing sounds do not change.
- **Type**: the noise to play. Eight built-in ones, in this menu order (new ones are only added at the end): **White**, **Pink**, **Brown** (from bright to dark hiss), **Crackle** (sparse clicks, vinyl-like), **Metal** (a ringing, inharmonic noise), **Digital** (bit-crushed, stepped noise), **Air Burst** (a short bright burst) and **Thump** (a short low noise hit). Then **User**, your own WAV loaded with **Load...**.
- **Level**: how loud, from silent to +12 dB. Default 0 dB.
- **Pitch**: plays the noise faster (higher) or slower (lower), -48 to +48 semitones. Default 0.
- **Key Track**: when on, the noise also moves with the note you play (one octave per octave, relative to C3); off by default, so the noise sounds the same on every note.
- **One Shot**: when on, the sound plays once from its start and stops; when off (default) it loops for as long as the kick sounds. Air Burst and Thump are meant for One Shot.
- **Phase**: where in the sound each kick starts, 0 to 100 %.
- **Random**: how much the start point varies from kick to kick (0 % always the same, 100 % anywhere).

The noise follows the **amp envelope** and the **Punch/Tail levels** like the oscillators (question 3.2 A), but **not the pitch envelope** (question 3.3 A). It has its own channel in the mixer ([mixer-and-buses](mixer-and-buses.md)). In the future, separate envelopes per source may come with a modulation system (owner's note, 2026-10-16); not in this version.

### Your own noise sample

**Load...** opens a WAV with the same rules as the Tock's user sample ([tock](tock.md)), up to 10 seconds. It is saved inside the project (name and audio, never the path), and choosing **User** with nothing loaded plays White.

## 2. User interface

The **Noise** panel takes the box of the bottom row that was kept for the LFOs (300 x 180 points, [ui-grid-layout](ui-grid-layout.md)); the LFOs will find a place when they are planned. Row 1: On, the Type menu, Load... Row 2: switches Key Track and One Shot. Row 3: knobs Level, Pitch, Phase, Random.

## 4. DSP / implementation approach

- `fcm::dsp::NoisePlayer` (in `src/dsp/noise/`) plays a published `NoiseSample` (mono float samples, sample rate, a loop flag) with linear interpolation at the rate `2^((pitch + keyTrack * (note - 60)) / 12) * sampleRateOfSample / engineSampleRate`. Loop mode wraps around with a 5 ms crossfade baked into the sample at build time (so the wrap never clicks); One Shot stops at the end. Start at note-on = `phase + random * u` (u from a small deterministic generator, reseeded by `reset()`), wrapped to the length.
- Factory noises (`fcm::dsp::FactoryNoises`) are generated by code at 48 kHz, 2 seconds each for the loops (White, Pink with the Voss-McCartney or Paul Kellet filter, Brown as integrated white with a leak, Crackle as sparse random impulses through a short decay, Metal as white noise through a bank of inharmonic resonators, Digital as sample-and-held, 4-bit noise), and 150 ms one-shots (Air Burst: high-passed noise with a fast decay; Thump: low-passed noise with a fast decay). All peak-normalised to the same peak, built off the audio thread, original, free to redistribute.
- Selection and publishing follow the Tock and wavetable pattern ([ADR 0008](../decisions/0008-wavetable-selection-and-publishing.md)): `core::NoiseSelector` publishes a `PublishedPointer<NoiseSample>`; the voice reads it once per block.
- User sample: `core::importNoiseSample` shares the WAV import of the Tock (same checks, same 10-second limit, resampled to 48 kHz when the file's rate is higher, so a stored sample is at most 480000 samples). Saved as node `UserNoiseSample` (`name`, `sampleRate`, `samples` in base64 float32), restored as untrusted input through `core::decodeStoredFloats`.
- In the voice the noise is a fourth stem: noise × level × amp envelope × Punch/Tail gain ([mixer-and-buses](mixer-and-buses.md)). The fading copy of a retriggered kick keeps playing its noise for its 1.5 ms.
- Real-time: nothing allocates, locks or waits.

## 5. Tests

- Factory noises: eight in the documented order, finite, same peak, deterministic; the loops have no step at the wrap; Pink and Brown have the expected spectral slopes (about -3 and -6 dB per octave within 1.5 dB over 100 Hz to 10 kHz).
- Player: Pitch +12 doubles the playback rate; Key Track moves it by one octave per 12 notes and does nothing when off; One Shot stops at the end, Loop never stops; Phase sets the start; Random 0 gives identical kicks, Random 100 gives different starts; `reset()` makes runs bit-identical; finite at extreme settings and sample rates 44.1, 48, 96 kHz.
- Voice: with Noise off the output is bit-identical to before; on, it follows the amp envelope and the Punch/Tail levels and ignores the pitch envelope.
- User sample: the Tock's import tests run for the noise importer too; state round trip; hostile nodes refused.
- Parameters: IDs, ranges and defaults in the released-ID guard.
